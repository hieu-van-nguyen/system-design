Requirements & Estimations

lesson
⏱ 8 min
📖 Chapter 22, Lesson 1

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Derive the upload storage math: 500 hrs/min × 1,440 min/day × 21 transcoded versions → 16.6 PB/day and why this forces a DAG transcoding architecture
  • Calculate aggregate CDN egress (1B hrs/day × 5 Mbps avg = 7.25 TB/s) and explain why this single number makes AV1 codec savings worth $2B+/year
  • Identify the four hard problems unique to video platforms: transcoding latency, adaptive bitrate, CDN economics, and recommendation target selection (watch time vs CTR)
  • Scope YouTube vs Netflix differences: UGC upload pipeline vs licensed content ingest, algorithmic recommendation vs curated catalog, DASH vs HLS

Step 1 — Clarifying Questions

YouTube and Netflix are architecturally similar at the delivery layer but diverge significantly at ingest (user-generated vs. licensed content) and discovery (algorithm vs. browse). Scope both differences explicitly.

Step 4 — Back-of-Envelope Estimation

Upload Volume

Upload rate:              500 hours of video / minute
Videos per minute:        500 hrs × 60 min/hr ÷ 30 min avg video = 1,000 videos/min
Videos per day:           1,000 × 1,440 min/day                  = 1.44M videos/day

Raw storage per video (30 min @ 1080p H.264):
  1,080p H.264 bitrate:  ~8 Mbps = 1 MB/sec
  30 min raw:            30 × 60 × 1 MB                          = 1.8 GB/video
  Daily raw ingest:      1.44M × 1.8 GB                          = 2.6 PB/day raw

After transcoding (21 versions per video, avg 550 MB each):
  Transcoded per video:  21 × 550 MB                             = 11.55 GB/video
  Daily transcoded:      1.44M × 11.55 GB                        = 16.6 PB/day
  5-year storage:        16.6 PB × 365 × 5 (+ 3× replication)   ≈ 90 PB (post-dedup ≈ 54 EB raw)

CDN Egress — The Most Important Number

Watch volume:             1 billion hours / day
Average playback bitrate: 5 Mbps (mix of 360p–1080p globally)
Daily CDN egress:         1B hrs × 3600 sec × 5 Mbps ÷ 8        = 2.25 PB/day
Aggregate bandwidth:      2.25 PB ÷ 86,400 sec                   = 26 GB/sec avg
Peak (4× average):        26 × 4                                 = 104 GB/sec peak

At commercial CDN rates ($0.02/GB): 2.25 PB/day × $0.02           = $45M/day
(YouTube uses GGC at ISPs, reducing this by ~80%: ~$9M/day)

AV1 savings math:
  AV1 = 50% smaller than H.264 at same quality
  50% bitrate reduction on 26 GB/sec avg:                         = 13 GB/sec saved
  At $0.02/GB × 86,400 sec/day:                                   = $22.5M/day = $8.2B/year
  (Realistic: mix of AV1 + VP9 + H.264 deployed; combined savings ≈ $2–4B/year)

Concurrent Viewers

1B hrs watched/day ÷ 24 hrs ÷ avg session 30 min = 83M concurrent average
Peak (prime time, multiple time zones): ~200M concurrent viewers

Per-viewer connection: 1 HTTPS session to CDN edge node
CDN edge servers (1 Gbps NIC, 5 Mbps avg per viewer): 1 Gbps ÷ 5 Mbps = 200 viewers/server
Servers for 200M concurrent: 200M ÷ 200 = 1M CDN edge servers (matches Google's reported fleet)

Transcoding Capacity

1,000 videos/min uploaded → must transcode before publishing
21 versions × 30-min video at 0.5× real-time encode speed (GPU) = 21 × 15 min = 315 min sequential
DAG parallel: all 21 × 54 segments = 1,134 tasks in parallel → wall clock = 1 task time = ~30 seconds
GPU transcoding cluster (each GPU handles 10 parallel encode jobs):
  1,000 videos/min × 21 versions = 21,000 encode tasks/min = 350 tasks/sec
  GPU throughput at 10 tasks/GPU: 350 ÷ 10 = 35 GPUs minimum (in practice 100s for burst + queue)

The Four Hard Problems

  1. Transcoding latency: A 90-min video has 540 ten-second segments. Sequential encoding takes 90+ minutes. DAG-parallel encoding reduces wall-clock to <60 seconds — the key architectural insight for upload pipeline.
  2. Adaptive bitrate without rebuffering: Network conditions fluctuate; the player must switch quality seamlessly using pre-segmented video + client-side buffer management (DASH/HLS manifests).
  3. CDN economics at 26 GB/s: Content-addressed immutable segment URLs enable max-age=1 year CDN caching; GGC/OCA at ISPs eliminates backbone egress; AV1 saves $2B+/year. All three must be mentioned.
  4. Recommendation target selection: Optimizing for CTR → clickbait epidemic. Watch-time regression prevents rabbit holes (long watch of low-quality content). Satisfaction signal (surveys + thumbs) is the third signal. The interviewer expects you to call out why CTR alone is wrong.

Key Takeaways

  • 16.6 PB/day transcoded output from 1.44M daily uploads × 21 versions — this is why transcoding needs a DAG, not a sequential pipeline
  • 26 GB/s aggregate CDN egress — the number that makes AV1's 50% bandwidth reduction worth $2–8B/year
  • 200M concurrent viewers × 5 Mbps = 1M CDN edge servers — content must live at the edge, not origin
  • Watch time > CTR: optimizing CTR creates clickbait; watch-time + satisfaction signal rewards genuinely good content

Interview Tips

  • State the DAG insight early: "500 hrs/min × 21 versions = sequential transcoding takes hours; DAG-parallel transcoding completes in under 60 seconds"
  • The AV1 savings math is a high-signal answer: "1B hrs/day × 5 Mbps × 50% AV1 reduction = 13 GB/s saved × $0.02/GB = $22.5M/day" — do this calculation live
  • Distinguish content-addressed URLs from regular URLs: "because URLs encode content hash, segments are immutable — CDN can cache max-age=1 year without stale content risk"
  • When asked about recommendation: lead with "we optimize watch_time × watch_fraction, not CTR — CTR creates incentive for misleading thumbnails that users immediately close"
