Requirements & Estimations

lesson
⏱ 8 min
📖 Chapter 18, Lesson 1

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Ask the right clarifying questions to scope an Instagram design in 3–5 minutes
  • State functional and non-functional requirements with specific targets (latency, availability, scale)
  • Calculate QPS, storage, and bandwidth from first principles for 2B MAU
  • Identify the three core scalability challenges: fan-out, media serving, and CDN architecture

Clarifying Questions

Spend the first 3–5 minutes asking these questions before drawing anything. They define scope and reveal the hardest design challenge upfront.

QuestionAssumed Answer
"Are we designing the full Instagram platform?"Core: photo/video posts, Stories, home feed, follow, likes, comments, Explore
"Do we need Reels (short-form video)?"Yes — same pipeline, shorter (up to 60s feed video, 15min Reels)
"Do we need Direct Messages?"Out of scope — different architecture (real-time messaging)
"Do we need Shopping / Ads?"Out of scope
"What is the target scale?"2B MAU, 500M DAU, global product
"Chronological or ranked feed?"Both: "Following" tab (chronological) and "Home" tab (ML-ranked)
"Do private accounts exist?"Yes — follow requests, access control at read time

Back-of-Envelope Estimation

State these numbers with confidence. Interviewers want to see the math and the reasoning, not just the final answer.

Users & Content Volume

Monthly Active Users (MAU):         2 billion
Daily Active Users (DAU):           500 million
Photos uploaded/day:                100 million
Videos (Feed/Reels) uploaded/day:    20 million
Stories posted/day:                 500 million

Avg posts viewed per user/day:       50 posts
Total feed reads/day:         500M × 50 = 25 billion

QPS (Queries Per Second)

WRITES (uploads):
  Photos:    100M / 86,400 = 1,157 uploads/sec  (avg)
  Videos:     20M / 86,400 =   231 uploads/sec
  Stories:   500M / 86,400 = 5,787 stories/sec
  Total:     ~7,200 upload writes/sec avg; ~20K/sec peak

READS (feed + media):
  Feed API:   25B / 86,400 = 289,000 reads/sec  (avg)
  Media CDN:  25B posts × 3 variants = 75B CDN req/day
              75B / 86,400 = 868,000 CDN requests/sec
  (→ >99% from cache; origin sees ~8,700 req/sec)

SOCIAL INTERACTIONS:
  Likes:     4.2B / 86,400 =  48,600 likes/sec
  Comments:  500M / 86,400 =   5,787 comments/sec

Storage

PHOTOS (per upload):
  Original: avg 5 MB (10 MP JPEG)
  Processed WebP variants: ~2 MB total stored
  Daily: 100M × 2 MB = 200 TB/day

VIDEOS (Reels/Feed):
  Original: avg 200 MB → transcoded to all qualities: 100 MB/video
  Daily: 20M × 100 MB = 2 PB/day

STORIES:
  500M × 0.5 MB avg = 250 TB/day
  (expire after 24h, retained 30 days for legal; S3 lifecycle deletes)

TOTAL NEW STORAGE/DAY: ~2.5 PB
5-year cumulative: 2.5 PB × 365 × 5 = 4.56 EB (exabytes)
  Tiering: hot (S3 Standard) → warm (S3-IA) → cold (S3 Glacier)
  Glacier after 90 days saves ~60% of storage costs

Bandwidth

INBOUND (uploads):
  7,200 req/sec × 2 MB avg = 14.4 GB/s inbound
  *** This CANNOT flow through API servers ***
  → Must use S3 presigned URLs (clients upload directly to S3)
  → API servers only handle metadata, not bytes

OUTBOUND (CDN serves):
  Feed API: 289K req/sec × 5 KB JSON = 1.4 GB/s
  CDN media: ~500 GB/s peak (distributed across 300+ PoPs globally)
  → 99%+ absorbed at CDN edge; origin sees <1%
The 14.4 GB/s upload insight: This is the number that forces the presigned URL architecture. At $0.09/GB transit cost, routing 14.4 GB/s through your own API servers costs $112,320/day in bandwidth alone — before server costs. S3 presigned URLs route data directly from client to S3, completely bypassing your API tier.

Key Takeaways

  • Instagram is a 500:1 read-heavy system — every architectural decision should optimize reads
  • The defining scalability challenge is fan-out: 1 post from a 600M-follower account → 600M Redis writes
  • Uploads must use S3 presigned URLs — 14.4 GB/s cannot flow through application servers
  • Five-year storage is 4.56 exabytes — tiered storage with lifecycle rules is mandatory, not optional
  • Stories' 24-hour TTL maps perfectly to Redis EXPIRE — eliminates the need for a separate expiry job
  • Media CDN is the highest-leverage infrastructure investment — 99%+ of all bytes must be served from edge, not origin

Interview Tips for This Section

  • Lead with "Instagram is extremely read-heavy — 500:1 read-write ratio" — this frames every subsequent decision
  • Calculate the storage and QPS numbers out loud; write them on the whiteboard so both you and the interviewer can reference them
  • Explicitly call out the 14.4 GB/s problem when discussing uploads — it's a filter question that separates senior from mid-level candidates
  • Mention that fan-out is the core design challenge before the interviewer asks — it shows you understand the domain
